Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose the best budget?

Start with the default budget of 0.5. If you need higher accuracy and can allocate more training time, increase budget to 1.0 or higher. For rapid prototyping, 0.1 to 0.3 is often sufficient. Because budget controls both step size (\(\alpha = 10^{-\text{budget}}\)) and internal generalization thresholds, increasing budget monotonically increases model capacity until the internal generalization control automatically halts splitting.

How does Perpetual prevent overfitting without a separate validation set?

At every candidate node split, PerpetualBooster partitions node samples into internal training and validation folds and evaluates the second-order Taylor loss reduction on both. A split is only accepted when the validation loss reduction keeps pace with the training loss reduction (\(\text{generalization} \geq 1.0\)). See How PerpetualBooster Works and PerpetualBooster Algorithm: How It Works in Architecture for details.

Does Perpetual support categorical features and missing values?

Yes. You can specify categorical columns via the categorical_features parameter without one-hot encoding. Missing values (NaN / None) are also handled natively during split finding via learnable missing branches.

Is Perpetual faster than LightGBM, XGBoost, and AutoGluon?

PerpetualBooster is implemented in Rust with Rayon data parallelism. Because it eliminates multi-trial hyperparameter optimization (such as running 100 Optuna trials), end-to-end training is 76x–148x faster than tuned LightGBM pipelines at matching accuracy (100x Faster Initial Training) and 3.1x faster on average than AutoGluon across the 104 OpenML AutoML benchmark classification datasets.

How do I update a trained model on new batches of data?

Initialize PerpetualBooster(reset=False) and call fit with the cumulative dataset as new batches arrive. Perpetual reuses existing trees and only fits new trees to the residual errors, reducing cumulative training complexity from \(O(n^2)\) to \(O(n)\). See Continual Learning and Further Speed-Up with Continual Learning.
